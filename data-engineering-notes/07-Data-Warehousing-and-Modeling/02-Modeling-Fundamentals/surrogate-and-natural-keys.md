# Surrogate Keys vs Natural Keys in Data Warehousing

## 1. Introduction

Keys are used to uniquely identify records and establish relationships between tables.

In data warehousing, two important types of keys are:

- **Natural Key:** An identifier that comes from the business or source system.
- **Surrogate Key:** An artificial identifier generated by the data warehouse.

Both are important in dimensional modeling, especially when building fact and dimension tables.

### Simple Example

Suppose an e-commerce company has a customer:

| customer_id | customer_name |
|---|---|
| C101 | Rahul |

Here, `C101` is the natural key because it comes from the source system.

The data warehouse may generate another identifier:

| customer_key | customer_id | customer_name |
|---:|---|---|
| 1001 | C101 | Rahul |

Here:

- `customer_id` is the natural key.
- `customer_key` is the surrogate key.

The surrogate key is used internally by the warehouse to identify the dimension record.

---

## 2. What Is a Natural Key?

A **Natural Key** is an identifier that already exists in the business domain or source system and has business meaning.

It is also called a business key.

### Examples

| Entity | Natural Key |
|---|---|
| Customer | Customer ID |
| Employee | Employee Number |
| Product | SKU |
| Country | Country Code |
| Order | Order Number |
| Bank Account | Account Number |

### Example: Customer Table

| customer_id | customer_name | email |
|---|---|---|
| C101 | Rahul | rahul@example.com |
| C102 | Priya | priya@example.com |
| C103 | Amit | amit@example.com |

Here, `customer_id` is the natural key.

It is assigned by the source application and identifies the customer in that business system.

### Characteristics

1. Comes from a business process or source system.
2. Has business meaning.
3. May already be used across applications.
4. Can be a single column or a combination of columns.
5. May change if business rules or source systems change.
6. May not be globally unique across multiple source systems.

### Important Point

A natural key is not necessarily a single column.

For example, a source system may identify a record using a combination of:

```text
(source_system, customer_id)
```

If two source systems both contain customer ID `101`, the combination can distinguish them.

---

## 3. What Is a Surrogate Key?

A **Surrogate Key** is an artificial identifier generated by a database or data warehouse to uniquely identify a record.

It has no direct business meaning.

### Example: Customer Dimension

| customer_key | customer_id | customer_name | city |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1001 | C101 | Rahul | Kolkata |
| 1002 | C102 | Priya | Delhi |
| 1003 | C103 | Amit | Mumbai |

Here:

- `customer_key` is the surrogate key.
- `customer_id` is the natural key.

The warehouse generates `customer_key` and uses it to connect fact records to the correct customer dimension record.

### Characteristics

1. Generated internally by the warehouse.
2. Usually has no business meaning.
3. Can be an integer, UUID, or another generated identifier.
4. Remains independent of source-system identifiers.
5. Helps manage historical dimension records.
6. Provides consistent warehouse relationships.

### Common Generation Methods

Surrogate keys can be generated using:

- Database identity columns.
- Sequences.
- Auto-increment columns.
- UUIDs.
- Deterministic hash-based identifiers.

The generation method depends on the warehouse platform and architecture.

## 4. Natural Key vs Surrogate Key

| Aspect | Natural Key | Surrogate Key |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Business meaning | No direct business meaning |
| Source | Source system or business domain | Generated by warehouse |
| Generation | Business application or source | Database or data pipeline |
| Uniqueness | May be unique within one source | Designed to be unique within its key scope |
| Stability | May change | Usually remains stable |
| Data type | String, integer, or composite | Integer, UUID, or hash |
| Historical tracking | Can identify the business entity | Can identify a particular dimension version |
| Usage | Source matching and business identification | Warehouse relationships and joins |

### Example

| customer_key | customer_id | customer_name |
|---:|---|---|
| 1001 | C101 | Rahul |
| 1002 | C102 | Priya |

- `customer_id` identifies the customer in the source system.
- `customer_key` identifies the warehouse dimension record.

**Remember:** Natural keys identify business entities. Surrogate keys identify warehouse records.

---

## 5. Why Do Data Warehouses Use Surrogate Keys?

Surrogate keys are particularly important in dimensional modeling.

### 5.1 Source-System Independence

Suppose a company receives customer data from two source systems.

**CRM System**

| customer_id | customer_name |
|---|---|
| 101 | Rahul |

**E-Commerce System**

| customer_id | customer_name |
|---|---|
| 101 | Priya |

Both systems use `101`, but they represent different customers.

Using the natural key alone can create a collision.

The warehouse can assign separate surrogate keys:

| customer_key | source_system | customer_id | customer_name |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1001 | CRM | 101 | Rahul |
| 1002 | E-Commerce | 101 | Priya |

This allows the warehouse to distinguish records from different source systems.

### 5.2 Supports Historical Tracking

One of the most important uses of surrogate keys is managing Slowly Changing Dimensions, especially SCD Type 2.

Suppose Rahul moves from Kolkata to Delhi.

The warehouse wants to preserve the historical customer information.

**Customer Dimension**

| customer_key | customer_id | customer_name | city | start_date | end_date | is_current |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1001 | C101 | Rahul | Kolkata | 2025-01-01 | 2026-05-31 | false |
| 1002 | C101 | Rahul | Delhi | 2026-06-01 | NULL | true |

Notice:

- The natural key remains `C101`.
- The surrogate key changes from `1001` to `1002`.
- The old dimension version is preserved.
- The new version represents the current customer information.

This allows historical sales to remain associated with the correct customer version.

### 5.3 Simplifies Fact Table Relationships

Fact tables can store compact surrogate keys instead of long business identifiers.

Example:

**fact_sales**

| order_id | customer_key | sales_amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| 101 | 1001 | 5000 |
| 102 | 1002 | 3000 |

The fact table connects directly to the appropriate customer dimension version.

### 5.4 Handles Source-System Changes

If a source system changes its customer identifier, the warehouse can retain its own surrogate key and manage the new source identifier through mapping logic.

### 5.5 Supports Consistent Dimensional Modeling

A warehouse can use a common key strategy across multiple source systems and fact tables.

This helps when building conformed dimensions.

## 6. Surrogate Keys and SCD Type 2

This is one of the most important interview topics.

**SCD Type 2** creates a new dimension record whenever a tracked attribute changes and historical preservation is required.

### Example

Initially, customer C101 lives in Kolkata.

Later, the customer moves to Delhi.

**Before the Change**

| customer_key | customer_id | city |
|---:|---|---|
| 1001 | C101 | Kolkata |

**After the Change**

| customer_key | customer_id | city | is_current |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1001 | C101 | Kolkata | false |
| 1002 | C101 | Delhi | true |

The surrogate key makes it possible for two records to represent different historical versions of the same business entity.

### Fact Table Relationship

Suppose Rahul made two purchases:

| order_id | customer_key | sales_amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| 101 | 1001 | 5000 |
| 205 | 1002 | 3000 |

The first purchase references the Kolkata version.

The second purchase references the Delhi version.

This preserves historical reporting.

### Important Distinction

- Natural key identifies the business entity across its versions.
- Surrogate key identifies an individual warehouse dimension version.

## 7. SQL Example: Creating Surrogate Keys

### Example 1: Identity Column

The following syntax is supported by databases such as SQL Server. Identity syntax differs across platforms.

```sql
CREATE TABLE dim_customer (
    customer_key INT IDENTITY(1,1) PRIMARY KEY,
    customer_id VARCHAR(50),
    customer_name VARCHAR(100),
    city VARCHAR(100)
);
```

The database generates `customer_key` when a new row is inserted.

### Example 2: PostgreSQL Identity Column

```sql
CREATE TABLE dim_customer (
    customer_key BIGINT GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    customer_id VARCHAR(50),
    customer_name VARCHAR(100),
    city VARCHAR(100)
);
```

### Example 3: Snowflake Sequence

```sql
CREATE SEQUENCE customer_key_seq
START = 1
INCREMENT = 1;

SELECT customer_key_seq.NEXTVAL;
```

A sequence can generate values for a surrogate key.

Exact implementation depends on the database platform.

## 8. Natural Keys and Surrogate Keys in ETL/ELT Pipelines

A typical dimensional data pipeline may look like this:

```text
Source System
      |
      v
   Bronze
      |
      v
   Silver
      |
      v
Match Natural Keys
      |
      v
Generate / Resolve Surrogate Keys
      |
      v
Load Dimension Tables
      |
      v
Load Fact Tables
```

### Typical Processing Steps

1. Read source records.
2. Identify the natural or business key.
3. Check whether the business entity already exists in the dimension.
4. Resolve the corresponding surrogate key.
5. Detect changes to tracked attributes.
6. Apply the required SCD strategy.
7. Load fact records using the appropriate surrogate keys.

### Important: Unknown and Missing Dimensions

Sometimes a fact record arrives before its corresponding dimension record.

A warehouse may use an inferred member or an unknown dimension record, depending on the design.

For example:

| customer_key | customer_id | customer_name |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | UNKNOWN | Unknown Customer |

The fact record can temporarily reference the unknown member.

When the actual customer information arrives, the pipeline can resolve or update the dimension according to the warehouse's late-arriving dimension strategy.

The value `0` is only an example. The actual unknown-key convention is a design choice.

## 9. Surrogate Keys in PySpark

A Data Engineer may need to generate or resolve surrogate keys while preparing dimension data.

### Example: Generating a Sequential Key for a Small Dataset

```python
from pyspark.sql import functions as F
from pyspark.sql.window import Window

window_spec = Window.orderBy("customer_id")

dim_customer = (
    customers
    .select("customer_id", "customer_name", "city")
    .withColumn(
        "customer_key",
        F.row_number().over(window_spec)
    )
)
```

This example assigns sequential numbers based on `customer_id`.

**Important limitations:**

- `row_number()` creates a sequential number within the current result.
- It does not provide a permanent key across independent pipeline runs.
- New or reordered records can receive different numbers.
- A stable tie-breaking order is important.
- This approach is suitable for illustrating key generation, not as a complete production SCD key-management strategy.

For production pipelines, maintain a persistent key mapping or use a reliable warehouse key-generation mechanism.

### Example: Resolving Surrogate Keys

Suppose a source sales dataset contains `customer_id`, while the dimension contains both natural and surrogate keys.

```python
fact_sales = (
    sales.alias("s")
    .join(
        dim_customer.alias("d"),
        F.col("s.customer_id") == F.col("d.customer_id"),
        "left"
    )
    .select(
        F.col("s.order_id"),
        F.col("d.customer_key"),
        F.col("s.sales_amount")
    )
)
```

For an SCD Type 2 dimension, matching only on `customer_id` is not sufficient because multiple historical versions may exist.

The pipeline must resolve the correct version using the applicable business event date and dimension validity dates.

## 10. Natural Keys vs Surrogate Keys in Fact Tables

In a typical dimensional model:

- Dimension tables use surrogate primary keys.
- Dimension tables retain natural keys for business identification and source matching.
- Fact tables store dimension surrogate foreign keys.
- Source business identifiers may also be retained for traceability.

### Example

**dim_product**

| product_key | product_id | product_name |
|---:|---|---|
| 10 | P01 | Laptop |
| 11 | P02 | Mouse |

**fact_sales**

| order_id | product_key | quantity | sales_amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 101 | 10 | 1 | 50000 |
| 102 | 11 | 2 | 1000 |

Here:

- `product_key` is the surrogate key.
- `product_id` is the natural key.
- `fact_sales.product_key` references `dim_product.product_key`.

The fact table may retain `order_id` as a source business identifier or degenerate dimension, depending on the model.

## 11. Common Interview Mistakes

| Incorrect Understanding | Correct Understanding |
|---|---|
| Natural keys are always unique across all systems | They may only be unique within one source system |
| Surrogate keys have business meaning | They are warehouse-generated identifiers |
| Surrogate keys replace natural keys completely | Natural keys are still useful for matching and traceability |
| Surrogate keys are used only for performance | They also support source independence and historical dimension versions |
| SCD Type 2 changes the natural key | The business key remains the same; a new surrogate key identifies the new version |
| Every pipeline can generate keys using `row_number()` | Production key management requires stable, persistent mappings |
| Surrogate keys always have to be integers | They can also be UUIDs or other generated identifiers |
| Fact tables should use source natural keys as dimension foreign keys | They commonly use dimension surrogate keys |

## 12. Interview Questions and Answers

### Q1. What is a natural key?

A natural key is an identifier that comes from the business domain or source system and has business meaning, such as customer ID, employee number, or product SKU.

### Q2. What is a surrogate key?

A surrogate key is an artificial identifier generated by the database or data warehouse to uniquely identify a record.

### Q3. What is the difference between a natural key and a surrogate key?

A natural key comes from the business or source system, while a surrogate key is generated internally by the warehouse and has no direct business meaning.

### Q4. Why do data warehouses use surrogate keys?

They provide source-system independence, support historical dimension tracking, simplify fact-to-dimension relationships, and help manage multiple source systems.

### Q5. Why are surrogate keys important in SCD Type 2?

SCD Type 2 creates new dimension records to preserve historical changes. Each version receives a different surrogate key while retaining the same natural business key.

### Q6. Can two records have the same natural key but different surrogate keys?

Yes. In an SCD Type 2 dimension, multiple historical versions of the same business entity share a natural key but have different surrogate keys.

### Q7. Can a natural key change?

Yes. Source-system migrations, business rule changes, and corrections can change natural identifiers. A warehouse surrogate key can remain stable independently.

### Q8. Should fact tables use natural keys or surrogate keys?

Fact tables commonly use surrogate keys as foreign keys to dimension tables. Natural keys may also be retained for traceability and reconciliation.

### Q9. How do you handle duplicate natural keys from multiple source systems?

Use a source-system identifier along with the natural key, or apply a governed entity-resolution process if the same real-world entity appears in multiple systems.

### Q10. Is a surrogate key always an integer?

No. It can be an integer, UUID, or another generated identifier. Integer keys are common in dimensional warehouses.

### Q11. Why is `row_number()` not always suitable for production surrogate key generation?

Because its generated numbers depend on the result set and ordering. New records or changed ordering can assign different numbers on later runs. Persistent key mapping is needed for stable identifiers.

### Q12. How do you handle a fact arriving before its dimension record?

Use an unknown or inferred dimension member according to the warehouse design, then resolve the fact-to-dimension relationship when the actual dimension information becomes available.

### Q13. What is the difference between a primary key and a surrogate key?

A primary key is a database constraint or designation that uniquely identifies a row. A surrogate key describes how an identifier is generated. A surrogate key is often used as a primary key, but the terms are not interchangeable.

### Q14. Can a natural key be a composite key?

Yes. A natural key may consist of multiple attributes, such as `(source_system, customer_id)`.

### One-Minute Interview Answer

A natural key is an identifier that comes from the source system or business domain, such as customer ID or product SKU. A surrogate key is an artificial identifier generated by the data warehouse.

For example, a customer may have source customer ID C101 and warehouse customer key 1001.

We use surrogate keys in dimensional modeling because source identifiers may change or conflict across systems. They also help manage historical dimension records.

The most important example is SCD Type 2. When a customer's city changes, we create a new dimension row with a new surrogate key while keeping the same natural key. Historical fact records continue referencing the old dimension version.

Therefore, natural keys identify business entities, while surrogate keys identify warehouse records or historical versions.
